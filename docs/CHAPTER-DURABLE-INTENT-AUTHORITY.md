# Opt-in durable Reader authority

`ChapterDemandOutbox.openWithDurableAuthority` enables a local, Reader-owned publication channel. It is distinct from the earlier immutable intent evidence: the companion can observe this channel while the Reader is closed, without opening the Reader's mutable journal. It is still an instantaneous current-state observation, not a worker lease, distributed lock or atomic audio-acceptance transaction.

The Reader owns both the existing account/device outbox lock and the publication-directory lock. An immutable, checksummed `authority-binding.json` inside the locked outbox pins the publisher UUID, account, requesting device and publication directory's actual device/inode identity. No paths or producer account epochs are serialized. Existing channels are not modified until both locks and that exact binding have been verified. The publisher lock's path remains bound to its owned descriptor during writes. A foreign, active, replaced or wrongly routed publication root fails closed.

First activation requires a fresh, existing private publication directory. The journal permanently uses V5 once enabled, including when empty and during acknowledgement-only saves. V5 checksums cover both entries and authority binding. Current plain writers reject the immutable mode binding; older V1–V4 writers reject journal V5. An activated journal cannot be downgraded to a checked legacy encoding and silently re-enabled. No user journals have been opened or migrated by validation.

Each intent/source/plan mutation follows this order:

1. Atomically write and synchronize a **blocked** `current.json`, including its parent directory, before changing the journal.
2. Write and synchronize the complete authoritative V5 journal.
3. Publish and synchronize a content-addressed, canonical archive of all complete wanted read sets.
4. Atomically publish and synchronize a **ready** pointer last.

Every transaction uses a new UUID nonce in its blocked and ready records. Identical archive content does not reuse that nonce. Acknowledgement or retained-audio-reference saves do not change wanted intent and may retain the head. Cancellation, re-request and attaching source/plan use the full barrier transaction.

The archive is limited to 8,192 entries and 32 MiB; the barrier is limited to 16 KiB. Each read set validates the original request, wanted revision and exact source/plan references. All archive entries must belong to the bound account/device and have strictly sorted, unique render keys. Files are copied/read back in 65,536-byte chunks with bounded autorelease pools, regular-file checks, descriptor/path/mtime fences and private read-only publication. Archives use no-replace linking and exact existing-content reconciliation. Reserved mutable barriers use atomic replacement. Unknown and historical archive files are preserved; aggregate retention/quota is still a service prerequisite.

`ChapterDurableIntentAuthority` implements the existing read-only authority protocol. The host supplies the authorized publication directory and SDK-created descriptor separately. The Worker opens only that publication root, checks its pinned identity, reads a ready barrier, verifies the immutable archive's hash/canonical contents, then rereads the **exact same barrier bytes**. A blocked, absent, changed or invalid channel cannot validate the read set. Archive references must be digest leaves before file access. The caller still wraps observations in the Worker's own current account-session gate, as `ChapterLocalIntentSnapshot.requireCurrent` does; directory identity is not an account credential.

Recovery takes both locks, verifies the immutable binding and blocks any existing channel **before** loading/replaying its journal. Missing, corrupt or downgraded activated journals stay blocked and fail; recovery never invents an empty queue. A successful recovery synchronizes V5 before republishing current read sets. Clean close preserves the last complete ready channel for offline observation.

Failures after blocking poison the Reader handle until close/reopen. Cleanup attempts another blocked write even when the task is cancelled, and never restores an older ready pointer. If the operating system refuses that cleanup write, the result remains explicitly ambiguous: the SDK cannot promise that a blocked marker was saved. A visible new ready pointer was published only after its new journal and archive were committed. Interrupted first activation after binding but before durable V5 also fails closed and preserves the files for separate recovery; it is not automatically treated as a legacy journal.

Cancellation checks follow ready publication and recovery/factory awaits before returning success. The factory joins its actor operation and blocks/closes on failed acceptance. Tests force failures/cancellation at blocked, journal, archive and ready boundaries, plus factory cancellation, then reopen to verify the authoritative journal outcome. They also exercise empty-V5/plain-writer rejection, valid-checksum downgrade rejection, missing/corrupt journals, both locks, directory identity, publisher-lock replacement, same-archive/new-nonce observations, and separate Reader close/offline reads. These are deterministic local filesystem/reopen tests, not actual power-loss, signed-process, App Group, cloud or model tests.

Generation/claim fencing and atomic acceptance of audio against current demand are unfinished. A cancellation may occur after an observation. Real service orchestration, aggregate quota, account/signed-host routing, prepared playback and live cloud qualification remain separate gates. This opt-in component does not activate the unavailable app backend or authorize model/phone workloads.
